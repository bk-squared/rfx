Use this recipe when a result fails a check: a non-passive `|S11| > 1`, a
resonance in the wrong place, a reflection or transmission that does not match
its reference, or a mesh series that does not converge. Go through the five
steps of the [overview](./overview) (declare, check on the grid, run, measure,
show the evidence) as checks: look at the result, the run and the model
before you change the model. The **STOP** entries are fixes that
were built and measured, and did not work; do not try one again without new
evidence. In this project, results of this kind have most often turned out to
be a mistake in the comparison or the extraction, not in the solver.

## First: look at the whole curve

Plot the full curve over frequency and compare it with something independent:
the expected sign, a closed-form magnitude, or a second run. A single pass or
fail number is not enough to start from.

A common trap: the dip in `|S11|` of a directly edge-fed patch looks like the
resonance. It is not; for a high-impedance edge feed the dip is the frequency
where the patch happens to match the feed. Read the resonance from a probe
after the source has stopped (`Result.find_resonances()`, see the
[resonance extraction recipe](./recipe-resonance-extraction)).

## Was the intended model solved? (step 2)

Read `sim.preflight()` and `sim.fidelity_report()` and quote every warning
before you report a number. A sheet solved wider than drawn, a port off its
metal or a structure in the absorber gives a wrong answer that looks like
physics ([Check the model on the grid](/rfx/check/model-on-grid/)).

## Was the run long enough? (steps 3 and 5)

Read the settling status of every result that reads frequencies:
`result.settling_witness["status"]` for a `run()` result, and each entry's
`["status"]` for an S-matrix result, which holds one per port. Anything other than `pass` means the run is not known to be long
enough ([Is the run long enough?](/rfx/check/simulation-time/)).

## Is the measurement right? (step 4)

1. `rfx.validate_port_smatrix(result)` checks passivity, reciprocity and the
   shape of any port result.
2. If you saved the port voltage and current records, replay them through the
   production assembly with `rfx.replay_smatrix_from_vi_dump(...)`. If the
   replay disagrees with your number, the mistake is in your comparison, not in
   the solver.

### A. Microstrip `|S11| > 1`

`compute_msl_s_matrix` returns the extracted S without clipping it, so an
excess reaches you. `result.sigma_max_excess` gives how far over 1 the largest
singular value goes at each frequency; `settling_db` and `reliable` point to the
cause. `enforce_passivity=True` bounds the matrix afterwards; it does not make
the corrected frequencies measurements. Check the probe placement first: the
default probe offset keeps clear of the field close to the source.

**STOP: a voltage-only fit of the wave amplitudes along the line.** At a
strongly mismatched resonant load the forward wave nearly vanishes at
resonance, the fit becomes ill-conditioned, and the extracted `|S11|` goes
above 1 and gets worse as the mesh is refined. A matched line is a separate
question that this does not answer.

### B. A resonance or dip in the wrong place

Measure the resonance from a probe after the source has stopped (see above).

**STOP: refining the mesh to move the dip of a microstrip patch.** The dip
moves towards a limit outside the expected band, and it is the matching
frequency, not the resonance.

**STOP: rotating the port voltage and current to the device edge with
tan(βd).** The rotation magnifies the error in the feed line's propagation
constant and gives inconsistent device impedances. Use the reference-plane
handling built into the S-parameter calculators.

### C. A rectangular waveguide `|S11|` that oscillates over frequency

**STOP: redesigning the waveguide port extraction for this.** The oscillation
seen on a PEC short came from a missing half-step time correction in a
diagnostic script, not in `compute_waveguide_s_matrix`. A phase disagreement
with another solver at a single frequency is usually a difference in
conventions; compare the trend over frequency. The magnitude error that remains is the grid's
own dispersion, which no change to the extraction removes.

## Does it converge with the mesh? (step 5)

### D. A curved metal body that does not converge

`conformal_pec=True` exists, but it currently makes a curved metal body
electrically larger and less accurate than the staircase: it gives cut edges a
raised permittivity instead of cut H-face areas. The update with cut H-face
areas (Dey–Mittra) is planned under tracker 1651. Until it lands, use the
staircase and a mesh series of at least three meshes, refined locally with a
graded mesh.

### E. "We need subgridding"

Subgridding is refused by default because it is not yet stable and verified.

**STOP: an SBP-SAT subgrid boundary.** Its boundary stencil does not close
under the leapfrog time step and does not work with CPML. Use a graded mesh for
local resolution
([Domain and mesh](/rfx/model/domain-mesh/)).

## None of the branches match

You may have found something new. Open an issue with the full curve over
frequency, the `validate_port_smatrix` output, every preflight warning and the
independent comparison from the first section. Try one fix; if it does not
close the gap, stop and rethink the approach instead of adjusting parameters.

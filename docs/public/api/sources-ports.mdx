This page lists the excitation calls, the port families and the S-parameter
calculator for each family. A **source** injects a field and has no
impedance. A **port** also terminates the line in a known impedance, so rfx can
compute S-parameters. Worked examples are in the
[Sources and Ports guide](/rfx/model/ports/) and
[Probes and S-Parameters](/rfx/measure/s-parameters/).

## Waveforms

`add_source()`, `add_polarized_source()`, `add_port()` and `add_msl_port()`
take a `waveform=` object. Waveguide ports take `f0` and `bandwidth` and
accept only `waveform="modulated_gaussian"`. TFSF sources take `f0`,
`bandwidth` and a pulse name (`"differentiated_gaussian"`,
`"modulated_gaussian"` or `"continuous_wave"`) or a `CustomWaveform`. Floquet
ports take `f0` and `bandwidth` only.

| Waveform | Signature | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `GaussianPulse` | `GaussianPulse(f0, bandwidth=0.5, amplitude=1.0, cutoff=3.0)` | default wideband pulse (differentiated Gaussian that starts from zero and has no DC) |
| `ModulatedGaussian` | `ModulatedGaussian(f0, bandwidth=0.5, amplitude=1.0, cutoff=5.0)` | band-pass pulse centred on `f0` |
| `CWSource` | `CWSource(f0, amplitude=1.0, ramp_steps=50)` | single frequency, ramped on over `ramp_steps` cycles of `f0` (not time steps), for steady-state fields |
| `CustomWaveform` | `CustomWaveform(func)` | your own `func(t) -> amplitude`, with `t` in seconds |

`bandwidth` is a fraction of `f0`. If you omit `waveform`, rfx uses a
`GaussianPulse` at `freq_max / 2`.

## Sources

```python
import numpy as np
from rfx import GaussianPulse, Simulation

sim = Simulation(freq_max=6e9, domain=(0.03, 0.03, 0.03), dx=1e-3,
                 boundary="cpml", cpml_layers=8)

sim.add_source((0.010, 0.015, 0.015), "ez",
               waveform=GaussianPulse(f0=3e9, bandwidth=0.8),
               amplitude_kind="current")
sim.add_polarized_source((0.010, 0.020, 0.015), polarization="slant45",
                         amplitude_kind="current")
```

| Method | Signature | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `add_source` | `add_source(position, component="ez", *, waveform=None, amplitude_kind=None)` | soft source on one E component at one grid edge; no load, so it suits resonance work (`find_resonances`) |
| `add_polarized_source` | `add_polarized_source(position, *, polarization="ez", waveform=None, amplitude_kind=None)` | linear polarization: `"ex"`, `"ey"`, `"ez"`, `"slant45"` or a real `(Ex, Ey)` tuple. For `"slant45"` or a tuple, each component gets a `GaussianPulse` with the waveform's `f0` and `bandwidth`, so pass a `GaussianPulse` |
| `add_tfsf_source` | `add_tfsf_source(*, f0=None, bandwidth=0.5, amplitude=1.0, margin=3, polarization="ez", direction="+x", angle_deg=0.0, waveform=None, method="bloch", closed_box=False, theta_deg=0.0, phi_deg=0.0, direction_tol_deg=0.1, max_component=100)` | plane wave travelling along x, for scattering, with CPML along x. Runs in a 3-D or a 2-D simulation (`Simulation(mode="2d_tmz")` or `"2d_tez"`); a closed box and a `CustomWaveform` need 3-D. `angle_deg` tilts the open (slab) wave in the transverse plane its polarization sets; `theta_deg` and `phi_deg` give a closed box's direction |
| `add_floquet_port` | see the [generated reference](/rfx/api/generated/) | experimental; no documented S-parameter API |

**`amplitude_kind`.** The default is `"current"`: the waveform amplitude is
a current moment I in A·m, injected as the current density I/dV over the
source cell (dV is the cell's volume), so E gains `Cb * I / dV` each step,
where Cb is the coefficient that also multiplies the curl of H in the E
update.
`"field"` adds the waveform directly to E each step. The signature sentinel
`None` also resolves to `"current"` and warns once per simulation; pass the
kind explicitly to silence that warning.
Tested in `tests/unit/sources/test_source_amplitude_kind.py`.

`add_polarized_source` also accepts `"rhcp"`, `"lhcp"` and complex tuples, but
circular polarization from this source has not been verified. Don't use it to
report axial ratio or sense.

For the plane wave on a graded mesh, only normal incidence along `+x` or `-x`
is valid. x may be graded: the incident line is built on the realized x cells.
A closed box or an oblique wave on a graded x is refused. Far-field and RCS use is covered in
[Far-Field and RCS](/rfx/measure/far-field-and-rcs/).

## Ports

| Method | Port family | Calculator |
|---|---|---|
| `add_port(...)` | lumped (one cell) or wire (`extent=`) | `run(compute_s_params=True)`, or `forward(port_s11_freqs=...)` for S11 gradients |
| `add_msl_port(...)` | microstrip line | `compute_msl_s_matrix()` |
| `add_waveguide_port(...)` | rectangular waveguide | `compute_waveguide_s_matrix()` |
| `add_coaxial_port(...)` | coaxial line | experimental uniform `run()` / `forward()`; `compute_coaxial_line_reflection()` (one-port), `compute_coaxial_two_port()` |

### Lumped and wire ports

```text
add_port(position, component="ez", *, impedance=50.0, waveform=None,
         extent=None, radius=None, excite=True, direction=None,
         reference_plane_cells=None, terminates=None) -> Simulation
```

- `impedance`: the port's reference impedance in ohms.
- `extent`: omit for a one-cell lumped port. Set it (in metres) to make a wire
  port spanning `extent` from `position` along the component axis.
- `radius`: the physical probe-pin radius for a wire port, subject to the
  local thin-probe mesh restrictions. With `radius=None`, the effective pin
  radius follows the mesh, so refining changes its series inductance.
- `excite=False`: a matched load only, no source. Use it for ports that only
  receive.
- `direction`: outward normal of the port (`"+x"`, `"-x"`, `"+y"`, `"-y"`),
  used to split V and I into incoming and outgoing waves. Detected from the
  nearest face if omitted.
- `terminates`: the conductor(s) this port ends, given as the shape or its
  index in the geometry list. A line should end at its ports, not in the
  absorber.
- `reference_plane_cells=N`: opt-in reference planes for a wire port's
  off-diagonal terms. See the [generated reference](/rfx/api/generated/).

```python
lp = Simulation(freq_max=6e9, domain=(0.03, 0.03, 0.03), dx=1e-3,
                boundary="cpml", cpml_layers=8)
lp.add_port((0.015, 0.015, 0.015), "ez", impedance=50.0,
            waveform=GaussianPulse(f0=3e9, bandwidth=0.8))
lp.add_lumped_rlc((0.015, 0.015, 0.016), "ez", R=50.0, C=1e-12,
                  topology="series")

print(lp.s_matrix_lane())                                   # run(compute_s_params=True)
print(lp.preflight_sparameters(calculator="run").ok)
result = lp.run(n_steps=600, compute_s_params=True,
                s_param_freqs=np.linspace(1e9, 5e9, 9))
print(result.s_params.shape)                                # (n_ports, n_ports, n_freqs)
```

`result.s_params[i, j, k]` is the response at port `i` with port `j` driven, at
frequency `result.freqs[k]`, with 0-based indices. The shortcuts count ports
from 1: `result.s11()` is `s_params[0, 0, :]`, and on a model with two or more
ports `result.s_db(2, 1)` is S21 in dB, from `s_params[1, 0, :]`. The mesh here is coarse for speed. For a real one-port S11,
start from about 60 cells per wavelength at the highest frequency of interest;
no current test measures the accuracy at that resolution, so a mesh series
decides it ([Is the mesh fine enough?](/rfx/check/mesh-convergence/)).

### Lumped R, L, C elements

```text
add_lumped_rlc(position, component="ez", *, R=0.0, L=0.0, C=0.0,
               topology="series") -> Simulation
```

Puts an R, L and/or C in one cell, updated with the fields. `topology` is
`"series"` or `"parallel"`. Set unused values to 0. A series element with only
one non-zero value is folded into the cell's material, so give at least two
to get the series circuit.

`add_lumped_rlc` is **not** a port. It embeds a circuit element in the FDTD
update, but it is not a reflection-referenced port: it fixes no reference
impedance, so it gives no Z0-referenced S11. To measure what a load
does to a line, drive with `add_port(..., impedance=Z0)` and place the
`add_lumped_rlc` element **a cell or more away from the port cell**, as the
example above does (it has no line, so it shows only the placement). An element on the port cell sits in parallel with the source,
and S11 does not see it.

For gradients with respect to R, L or C, pass
`forward(rlc_values_override={index: {"R": R, "L": L, "C": C}})` on the
uniform single-device path. That changes the element; it still does not make
it a port.

### Microstrip ports

```text
add_msl_port(position, *, width, height, direction="+x", impedance=50.0,
             waveform=None, excite=True, n_probe_offset=None,
             n_probe_spacing=None, n_probes=5, name=None, mode="laplace",
             eps_r_sub=None, terminates=None) -> Simulation
```

The port covers the full trace cross-section and fits the line's β and Z0 from
a short probe array downstream of the feed.

- `position = (feed, centre, z_substrate_bottom)`: the coordinate along
  `direction` is the feed plane; the other in-plane coordinate is the trace
  centre.
- `direction`: `"+x"`, `"-x"`, `"+y"` or `"-y"`. The substrate normal is always
  z.
- `impedance`: the port's reference impedance in ohms. S is referred to it;
  the line's analytic impedance is reported as `line_impedances`.
- `mode`: `"laplace"` (default, quasi-TEM) or `"uniform"`. `"eigenmode"`
  runs only for an x-directed port on a uniform mesh on one device, and its
  S-parameters are not validated.
- `n_probe_offset`, `n_probe_spacing`: in cells. Left at `None`, each is set
  from a length (the offset from the fringing length, the spacing from the
  guided wavelength at `freq_max`) and counted again at run time in the cells
  where the probes lie. On a mesh graded along the line, that is the uniform
  zone of cells beside the feed, when the probes stay inside it; otherwise the
  counts made at declaration are kept and a warning says so.

```python
from rfx import Box

from rfx.mesh_edges import edge_aware_profiles

h, w = 0.3e-3, 0.6e-3                     # 300 um substrate, 600 um trace
domain = (0.020, 0.008, 0.003)
strip = Box((0.003, 0.004 - w / 2, h), (0.017, 0.004 + w / 2, h))  # port to port
msl = Simulation(freq_max=10e9, domain=domain, dx=100e-6,
                 boundary="cpml", cpml_layers=8,
                 **edge_aware_profiles(domain, 100e-6, sheets=[strip],
                                       faces={"y": [0.004]}))
msl.add_material("ro4350b", eps_r=3.66)
msl.add(Box((0.0, 0.0, 0.0), (0.020, 0.008, h)), material="ro4350b")
msl.add_thin_conductor(Box((0.0, 0.0, 0.0), (0.020, 0.008, 0.0)))            # ground
msl.add_thin_conductor(strip)
msl.add_msl_port((0.003, 0.004, 0.0), width=w, height=h, direction="+x")
msl.add_msl_port((0.017, 0.004, 0.0), width=w, height=h, direction="-x",
                 excite=False)
print(msl.s_matrix_lane())                # compute_msl_s_matrix
```

The strip runs from port to port. A strip that continued behind a port would be
an open stub, which shorts the port at its quarter-wave frequency and the odd
multiples of it. The calculators refuse a stub with one of those frequencies
between the band's lowest frequency divided by 1.5 and 1.5 times its highest. A
tail short enough that its quarter-wave frequency lies above 1.5 times the
band's highest frequency passes, as the 3.25 mm tails behind the ports of
`examples/tutorials/ports_and_sparams_101.py` do for its band up to 6 GHz. Preflight takes a free sheet edge to lie 0.35 in-plane cell beyond the
sheet's last node, so the edge-aware in-plane mesh puts the 600 µm strip's last
nodes 0.35 cell inside its drawn side edges. `edge_aware_profiles` does not
know where the ports are, so it meshes the two ends as free edges too, with
the last node 0.35 cell inside each; a plane declared in `faces=` on a sheet
edge would be dropped with a warning, so the example declares only the trace's
centre line. The microstrip ports feed those ends over the trace's full width,
so preflight takes each end at its last node and leaves it out of the size
check. The 0.35 was measured where the cell
normal to the sheet is as long as the in-plane cell beside its edge. Here the
z cell is 100 µm and the in-plane cell beside each side edge 90 µm, a ratio of
1.11. For a free sheet in vacuum the offset at that ratio was measured as 0.37
to 0.38 cell (E perpendicular and parallel to the edge), so in vacuum this
strip would be 604 to 605 µm wide. On the substrate's top face, where the
strip lies, the offset has not been measured. On a uniform 100 µm mesh
preflight predicts 670 µm, and `run()` refuses the strip. A cell of 100 µm puts 3 cells across the 300 µm substrate, which is coarse for speed:
preflight warns below 4. For microstrip, the substrate thickness
sets the cell size, not the wavelength.

### Waveguide ports

```text
add_waveguide_port(x_position, *, x_range=None, y_range=None, z_range=None,
                   mode=(1, 0), mode_type="TE", direction="+x", freqs=None,
                   n_freqs=50, f0=None, bandwidth=0.5, amplitude=1.0,
                   probe_offset=10, ref_offset=3, calibration_preset=None,
                   reference_plane=None, probe_plane=None, name=None,
                   n_modes=1, waveform="modulated_gaussian",
                   mode_profile="discrete") -> Simulation
```

`x_position` is the port plane's coordinate along the axis named by
`direction` (for `direction="-y"` it is a y coordinate). The two `*_range`
arguments across that axis give the guide aperture. An aperture that spans
the whole cross-section needs PEC on the transverse faces and an absorber on
the port axis only (`boundary={"x": "cpml", "y": "pec", "z": "pec"}` for an
x-directed port); on a uniform mesh `boundary="cpml"` on every face is refused. A
smaller aperture needs a conductor on every edge: draw PEC walls there, or a bare
edge is refused on both meshes. Waveguide ports
need a 3-D simulation (`Simulation(mode="3d")`, the default), and cannot share a simulation with a lumped or wire port, or a soft source (`add_source`).

A port measures at `freqs`, or at `n_freqs` points from `freq_max/10` to
`freq_max` when `freqs` is not given (50 by default), on every path.
Every path that takes several waveguide ports (`forward()`, a graded `run()`
and `compute_waveguide_s_matrix()`) refuses ports whose bins differ: declare
the same `freqs`, or the same `n_freqs`, on every port. A uniform `run()` takes
one waveguide port.

```python
wg = Simulation(freq_max=12e9, domain=(0.10, 0.023, 0.010), dx=1e-3,
                boundary={"x": "cpml", "y": "pec", "z": "pec"}, cpml_layers=8)
band = np.linspace(8.2e9, 12.4e9, 11)
wg.add_waveguide_port(0.01, y_range=(0.0, 0.023), z_range=(0.0, 0.010),
                      direction="+x", freqs=band, name="p1")
wg.add_waveguide_port(0.09, y_range=(0.0, 0.023), z_range=(0.0, 0.010),
                      direction="-x", freqs=band, name="p2")
print(wg.s_matrix_lane())                 # compute_waveguide_s_matrix
```

The [Waveguide Ports guide](/rfx/guide/waveguide-ports/) covers calibration
planes and `normalize`.

### Coaxial ports

`add_coaxial_port(...)` builds a coax line, matched feed, TEM drive and voltage planes in experimental uniform single-device `run()` and `forward()`. `position` is the reference plane; `face="top"` or `"bottom"` selects the feed side and `pin_length` extends the pin toward the device. `run(compute_s_params=True, s_param_freqs=...)` and `forward(port_s11_freqs=...)` read S11 at that plane using the built line impedance, then renormalize it to `impedance`. The radii, `pin_length` and `impedance` are static declarations; PEC occupancy elsewhere can be differentiated. The coaxial calculators remain available with their own line layouts and limits.

Uniform mesh, one device, one coax port and no other S-port family; face top or bottom, float32, three-dimensional second-order Yee, CPML on all six faces, no periodic axis or TF/SF. The shell needs at least one whole cell from every lateral absorber, enough axial room for the line, and an NTFF box, when declared, containing the whole port. No conformal PEC, subpixel smoothing, eps_override or sigma_override.

Experimental uniform coax ports: same-line calculator equality, run()/forward() equality, bottom-feed, NTFF-leakage and occupancy-gradient checks define the validation scope; the antenna-level result against an independent solver is not yet shown.

```text
add_coaxial_port(position, face="top", *, pin_length=0.005,
                 pin_radius=0.000635, outer_radius=0.002055, impedance=50.0,
                 waveform=None, terminates=None) -> Simulation
```

The coaxial calculators build their own line, TEM source, probe planes and
termination. The port supplies the x/y centre, `face`, the two radii, the
waveform and `impedance`. `compute_coaxial_two_port` refers S to `impedance` and
reports the line's TEM impedance as `line_impedances`;
`compute_coaxial_line_reflection` returns Γ at the line and does not use
`impedance`.
See the complete example below.

## S-parameter calculators

| Calculator | Signature (key arguments) | Returns |
|---|---|---|
| lumped/wire | `run(..., compute_s_params=True, s_param_freqs=None, s_param_n_steps=None)` | `Result.s_params`, `Result.freqs` |
| lumped/wire, differentiable | `forward(..., port_s11_freqs=freqs)` | `ForwardResult.s_params`, shape (ports, ports, frequencies); only the driven ports' columns are filled, the others are complex NaN |
| microstrip | `compute_msl_s_matrix(*, n_steps=None, num_periods=40.0, freqs=None, n_freqs=100, enforce_passivity=False, eps_override=None, ...)` | `MSLSMatrixResult` |
| waveguide | `compute_waveguide_s_matrix(*, n_steps=None, num_periods=20.0, normalize=False, eps_override=None, sigma_override=None, strict_passivity=False, ...)` | `WaveguideSMatrixResult` |
| coax, one-port | `compute_coaxial_line_reflection(*, termination="short", n_steps=6000, freqs=None, n_freqs=11, cpml_axes=None, probe_count=12, probe_start_cells=8, probe_spacing_cells=4, feed_impedance=None, dut_impedance=None, eps_scale=None, ...)` | `CoaxialLineReflectionResult` |
| coax, two-port | `compute_coaxial_two_port(*, n_steps=6000, freqs=None, n_freqs=11, cpml_axes=None, probe_count=12, feed_impedance=None, eps_scale=None, ...)` | `CoaxialTwoPortResult` |

Result fields are listed on [Results & Observables](/rfx/api/results/#s-parameter-results).
`run(compute_s_params=True)` handles lumped and wire ports only. It does not
dispatch to the other families.

**Let rfx choose.** `sim.compute_s_matrix(method=None, **kwargs)` reads the
registered ports, calls the matching calculator and forwards your keyword
arguments unchanged. `sim.s_matrix_lane()` returns that choice as a string and
runs nothing. Two cases need you to decide:

- One coaxial port is ambiguous. Pass
  `method="compute_coaxial_line_reflection"` or `method="compute_coaxial_two_port"`
  (`lane=` still works with a deprecation warning).
- Lumped/wire-only models raise, naming `run(compute_s_params=True)`, because
  that call needs your `n_steps`.

**Cross-family calculators.** Their magnitudes are not validated. Measure each
side with its own calculator instead.

- `compute_mixed_s_matrix()` (lumped or wire plus microstrip) is experimental.
  By default (`enforce_passivity=True`) it clips a non-passive S to a passive
  one, and the clip changes the passive entries too.
- `compute_coax_msl_transition()` (coax plus microstrip) is **not supported
  (deprecated); removed after 2.3**. Its matrix is not passive, so by default
  (`strict_passivity=True`) it raises `ValueError`.

### Checking a calculator before the run

```text
preflight_sparameters(*, calculator="run", strict=False, normalize=None,
                      include_general=False) -> PreflightReport
```

`calculator` is `"run"`, `"forward"`, `"msl"` or `"waveguide"`. It checks the
registered ports against that calculator's port rules, without running FDTD.
Read `report.ok`. `strict=True` raises one `ValueError` listing the
error-severity findings only, because a correct waveguide setup always carries
an informational note; `Simulation.preflight(strict=True)` instead raises on
every finding. The report does not run every
check the calculator runs when it starts: an open stub behind a microstrip
port, for example, is refused by `compute_msl_s_matrix()` while this report
reads `ok`. Pass `include_general=True` to add the general preflight.

```python
print(msl.preflight_sparameters(calculator="msl").ok)
report = wg.preflight_sparameters(calculator="waveguide")
print(report.ok, [issue.code for issue in report])
```

For waveguides it also audits the setup:

- `record_shorter_than_far_boundary_round_trip` (error): the record is shorter
  than one round trip to the far boundary. Raise `num_periods`.
  `record_far_boundary_band_below_cutoff` means the lowest frequency is below
  the port's cutoff, so the check could not be made.
- `port_index_mirror_asymmetry`: an opposite-facing port pair is not placed
  symmetrically. A healthy two-port guide always shows the informational
  `port_index_mirror_known_e_plane_offset` note, so a non-empty report is not a
  failure.
- `layout_measured_from_band_low_edge` (information): the band's lowest bin is
  less than 6 % above the port's cutoff, where the guide wavelength changes
  fast with frequency. Size the distance to the far wall and the absorber from
  the guide wavelength at a frequency below the band, not at its lowest bin, and
  confirm with the record check above. On a WR-90 sweep, a box sized from the
  band's own lowest bin left the band's bottom bins unconverged, while the box
  sized for the next lower band read them within 3 %.

The coaxial calculators check their own requirements when called.

## Complete coaxial example

`compute_coaxial_line_reflection()` needs a float32, 3-D, second-order uniform
Yee grid with CPML on all six faces (positive thickness on both z faces),
no periodic axis, and exactly one `face="top"` coaxial port. The line absorbs
on all three axes (`cpml_axes="xyz"`, the only accepted value); its outer wall
must stay more than one cell from every lateral absorber.
Nothing else may be registered: it rejects extra geometry, sheets, RLC
elements, probes, monitors and NTFF boxes rather than ignoring them.

```python
coax = Simulation(freq_max=40e9, domain=(0.008, 0.008, 0.040), dx=0.35e-3,
                  boundary="cpml", cpml_layers=16)
coax.add_coaxial_port(
    (0.004, 0.004, 0.020),          # x, y are used; z is derived internally
    face="top",
    pin_radius=0.635e-3,
    outer_radius=2.055e-3,
    waveform=GaussianPulse(f0=8e9, bandwidth=1.2),
)
refl = coax.compute_coaxial_line_reflection(
    termination="short",
    probe_count=12,
    n_steps=4000,
    freqs=np.linspace(4e9, 12e9, 5),
)
print(refl.status, f"{refl.annulus_cells:.2f} annulus cells")
print(np.abs(refl.s11))
```

- `termination`: `"short"`, `"open"` or `"matched"`. With `"matched"`,
  `dut_impedance=R` makes the load a resistor R. Don't pass `dut_impedance`
  otherwise.
- `feed_impedance`: the feed resistor. `None` uses the line's analytic TEM
  impedance.
- `probe_count` must be at least 3, and every probe plane must fit between the
  load and the source; otherwise the call raises before running. The probe
  planes are placed in cells, so refining the mesh shortens the span it covers.
  Give it enough physical length.
- `dx = 0.35 mm` puts about 4 cells across the 1.42 mm annulus. Keep at least
  4. `status` reads `"under_resolved"` below about 3.5 and `"contaminated"`
  when the field is not a clean single TEM mode at some frequency. Check that it reads `"passed"`.
- The result does not pass through the shared passivity check. Inspect
  `status`, `recurrence_residual` and `s11` yourself.

## Limits

- **Resolution.** Lumped and wire ports: start from about 60 cells per
  wavelength at the highest frequency for one-port S11. No current test
  measures the accuracy at that resolution; a mesh series decides it
  ([Is the mesh fine enough?](/rfx/check/mesh-convergence/)). Coax: at least 4 cells across the annulus. Microstrip: at least 4 cells
  through the substrate (preflight warns below that).
- **Port reference plane.** A one-cell lumped or wire port reads V at its node
  and I half a cell toward the line.
- **End lines at their ports, not in the CPML.** CPML terminates a guided
  line much less well than a plane wave. Let the ports terminate the line.
- **Coax results from before the three-axis absorber** (`cpml_axes="z"`, now
  refused) came from a line in a closed metal can and do not reproduce.
- **Graded meshes.** Coaxial ports raise on a graded mesh. Lumped-port
  S-parameters come from `run(compute_s_params=True)` or
  `forward(port_s11_freqs=...)`. Microstrip and waveguide calculators run there but are
  experimental. See [Support](/rfx/support/).
- **Microstrip bins.** Exclude every frequency bin where any entry of
  `MSLSMatrixResult.reliable` is `False`: S is solved jointly across drives, so
  the whole slice `S[:, :, k]` is affected. A `True` entry does not guarantee
  accuracy.
- **Sources, probes and monitors are not ports.** They have no reference
  impedance, so they cannot produce S-parameters.

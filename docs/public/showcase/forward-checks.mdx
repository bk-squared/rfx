A gradient is only as useful as the simulation it differentiates. These two
boards are solved by rfx and by openEMS, and each comparison is a test in the
repository (`tests/crossval/rt5880_patch/` and `tests/crossval/msl_notch_filter/`).

<p data-rfx="note">The curves on this page were computed with rfx 1.8.0 (commit <code>b46a8220</code>),
before the settling check judged each frequency a result reads. The comparison has not been repeated under that
check, so this page gives no agreement figure. The notch filter's uniform-mesh ladder has not been shown to
converge on the drawn 600 µm line.</p>

## Probe-fed patch on RT/Duroid 5880

<figure data-rfx="media">
  <img src="https://remilab.cnu.ac.kr/share/52f0a897c27d/forward-patch/forward_curves_rt5880_patch_site.png" loading="lazy" alt="Real and imaginary parts of the input impedance of the RT/Duroid 5880 probe-fed patch from rfx and from openEMS on its finest mesh, 2.0 to 2.9 GHz" />
</figure>

The figure shows the real and imaginary parts of the input impedance from both
solvers. Neither model declares a probe radius, so each feed cell sets the
probe's series reactance; the two solvers' reactance curves differ for that
reason, and the case judges the resonance and the input resistance, not the
`|S11|` dip.

## Microstrip open-stub notch filter

<figure data-rfx="media">
  <img src="https://remilab.cnu.ac.kr/share/52f0a897c27d/forward-notch/forward_curves_msl_notch_filter_site.png" loading="lazy" alt="|S21| and |S11| of the microstrip open-stub notch filter from rfx and from openEMS on its finest mesh, 2 to 7 GHz" />
</figure>

A 12 mm open stub on a 50 Ω microstrip line (254 µm, εr 3.66) is a quarter wave
near 3.7 GHz, where it shorts the line. The figure shows |S21| and |S11| from
both solvers.

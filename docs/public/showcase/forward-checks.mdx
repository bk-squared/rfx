A gradient is only as useful as the simulation it differentiates. These two
boards are solved by rfx and by openEMS; each comparison is a test in the
repository that judges it on a converged mesh ladder, and the curves here were
computed at the same commit and on the same mesh as the tests' judged rung.

## Probe-fed patch on RT/Duroid 5880

<figure data-rfx="media">
  <img src="https://remilab.cnu.ac.kr/share/7c02ad43c580/forward-patch/forward_curves_rt5880_patch_site.png" loading="lazy" alt="Real and imaginary parts of the input impedance of the RT/Duroid 5880 probe-fed patch from rfx and from openEMS on its finest mesh, 2.0 to 2.9 GHz" />
</figure>

On a mesh of 12 cells through the substrate (52.7 M cells), the peak of
Re(Z_in) sits at 2.3172 GHz against openEMS's 2.3195 GHz (−0.097 %), and the
resistance there is 73.15 Ω against 73.46 Ω (−0.42 %); the test's bars are 1 %
and 2 %. The reactance is about 7 Ω higher in rfx (+50.3 against +43.0 Ω at
resonance) because neither model declares a probe radius, so each feed cell
sets the probe's series reactance. That offset makes the rfx |S11| minimum 5 dB
shallower (−15.4 against −20.6 dB) while its frequency differs by only 0.12 %.

## Microstrip open-stub notch filter

<figure data-rfx="media">
  <img src="https://remilab.cnu.ac.kr/share/7c02ad43c580/forward-notch/forward_curves_msl_notch_filter_site.png" loading="lazy" alt="|S21| and |S11| of the microstrip open-stub notch filter from rfx and from openEMS on its finest mesh, 2 to 7 GHz" />
</figure>

A 12 mm open stub on a 50 Ω microstrip line (254 µm, εr 3.66) is a quarter wave
near 3.7 GHz, where it shorts the line. On a mesh of 6 cells through the
substrate the notch is 0.090 % from the openEMS tutorial's finest mesh, and |S21|
agrees within 0.23 dB once the notches are aligned (bar 2 dB).

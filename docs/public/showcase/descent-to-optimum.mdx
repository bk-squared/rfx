A three-layer coating matches free space to a substrate of εr = 12 over
8–12 GHz. Each layer is 5.5 mm (11 cells) thick; the three permittivities are
the design variables and the objective is the band-mean power reflection. The
transfer-matrix method gives the exact optimum, so the question is whether
gradient descent through a time-domain solve lands there.

## What happened

From the geometric-ladder start (1.861 / 3.464 / 6.447) the layer permittivities
settle at 1.922 / 4.670 / 4.707 against the optimum 1.943 / 4.652 / 4.720,
although the closed-form optimizer started from a different point. Evaluated
with the transfer matrix, the design's band-mean |R|² is 0.03434 against the
optimum's 0.03430, −14.6 dB, 3.0 dB below the start. FDTD and
transfer-matrix |R|² of the final design differ by at most 1.5×10⁻³ over the 15
frequencies the cost uses (8.20–11.78 GHz).

## How the gradient was checked

Against central differences at the start to 5.5×10⁻⁴, and against a 1.5× longer
record to 4.8×10⁻⁴.

<p data-rfx="note">A one-dimensional stack at normal incidence; it shows the optimizer and the gradient work together, not the accuracy of a 3-D radome.</p>

The patch model above, on the mesh of 4 cells through the substrate, has 1.98
million cells and runs 9,912 time steps. On one RTX A6000 with rfx v2.0.0 a
forward run takes about 8.2 s. The value and the gradient with respect to
every cell of the design region take about 21 s, 2.5 times a forward run,
whether the region holds 12,400, 20,592 or 43,008 cells.


| Box | Cells | Forward (s) | Value + gradient (s) | Peak memory, value + gradient (GB) | Central differences (derived, days) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| patch footprint | 12,400 | 8.24 | 20.87 | 4.51 | 2.4 |
| design box | 20,592 | 8.25 | 20.94 | 6.86 | 3.9 |
| whole substrate layer (incl. 19,436 vacuum cells) | 43,008 | 8.25 | 21.09 | 13.61 | 8.2 |

Times are the median of three runs after compilation.

On the finest mesh, 12 cells through the substrate with 555,984 design cells
and 29,736 time steps, one NVIDIA H200 NVL takes 184 s for a forward run and
598 s, about 10 minutes and 3.25 times a forward run, for the value and
gradient with 21 checkpoint segments. Central differences would take about
6.5 years (derived).

## What grows instead

Memory. The reverse pass keeps a per-step record for the cells of the design
box, so peak device memory rises from 0.46 GB for a forward run to 4.5, 6.9
and 13.6 GB for the value and gradient on the 4-cell mesh. On the finest mesh, 21 checkpoint
segments hold it at 83 GB. For larger regions, [Reduce Memory Use](/rfx/run/memory-and-speed/)
explains checkpointing, which trades recomputation for memory.

<p data-rfx="note">The central-difference column is not measured: it is 2 × N × the measured forward time.</p>

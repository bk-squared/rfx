## What it is for

rfx is an open-source simulator with basic feature support, not production
software. Whether a result is accurate enough is decided by a stated bar, not
by how small a residual can be made. The project judges its own validation
cases by this bar, and you can judge your results by it too.

## How to use it

A comparison of an rfx result with a reference (a closed form, a measurement or
another solver) passes the bar when all of these hold:

1. **The mesh has converged.** The result was computed on at least three
   meshes and the trend is stated. A comparison on a mesh that has not been
   shown to converge says nothing either way: it is neither a pass nor a
   defect. See [Mesh convergence](/rfx/evidence/mesh-convergence/).
2. **The trend over frequency agrees.** The curve's shape against the
   reference — where its features are and how they move — agrees across the
   band. A single frequency, a band maximum or a band mean is not a verdict.
3. **Power and magnitude within 2 dB** of the reference, compared after the
   frequency axis is scaled so the judged feature lines up with the
   reference's (the frequency error is judged by item 4). Bins where either
   curve is below −20 dB (a deep null) are judged by position only.
4. **Frequencies within 1 %**: resonances, cutoffs, notches and nulls. A
   band edge defined by where a curve crosses a level (a −3 dB corner) is
   judged by the 2 dB magnitude bar instead: its frequency may move as far as
   2 dB along the reference curve's own slope there.
5. **Group delay within 1 %**: the least-squares slope of the unwrapped
   transmission phase against frequency, over the bins where |S21| is above
   −20 dB (for a one-port, the reflection phase). On a dispersionless line this
   equals the electrical length βl; on a microstrip or a waveguide the two
   differ, and the bar reads the group delay. The phase at a single frequency
   is not judged.
6. **Narrow features also against their bandwidth.** For a resonance, passband
   or notch whose fractional bandwidth is 5 % or less, the frequency error must
   also be within 10 % of the reference's bandwidth. For a filter with a 2 %
   bandwidth that means within 0.2 %, because 1 % of the centre frequency is
   half the passband.
7. **No verdict from one number.** A pass or a fail names the curve, the mesh
   statement and the reference together.

The same applies to the checks you make on your own run: whether the
[simulation time](/rfx/evidence/simulation-time/) is long enough, or the mesh
fine enough, is read from how the result moves over at least three run lengths
or meshes, and whether it moves the way the physics predicts.

A result inside the bar that converges with the mesh is not a defect. Some
structures cannot be judged this way because of what they are: a high-Q
resonance, a deep null, a phase-only comparison, or a reference that is itself
good to only a few percent. State that, and the criterion you used instead.

## What is checked

Items 1, 3, 4 and 5 are written in code, so a check cannot move the bar it is
judged by. Items 2, 6 and 7 are applied by the person reading the comparison;
no check implements them yet.

- `tests/crossval/_v2_judging.py` holds the frequency bar (1 %), the magnitude
  bar (2 dB), the deep-null level (−20 dB), the level-crossing rule, and the
  mesh statement: the last two meshes agree within 1 %, and every step larger
  than 0.1 % moves the same way. It judges a ladder of two or more meshes; the
  bar asks you to state the trend over three.
- `tests/_electrical_length.py` holds the group-delay bar (1 %) and its −20 dB
  level.
- The cross-solver cases under `tests/crossval/` compare rfx with frozen
  reference data from another solver, by this bar.

## How to read an accuracy statement

Pages on this site state accuracy one line at a time: the statement, its
[status](/rfx/support/) and the file in the repository that checks it. A number
with no such file is not printed. A clean preflight or a run that completes
does not move a feature's status.

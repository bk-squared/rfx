## What it is for

A source puts energy into the model without being part of the structure. rfx
has two kinds: a point source, which drives one field component at one cell
(a short current element), and a plane wave, which illuminates the structure
from one side. A source has no impedance and absorbs nothing, so it gives no
S-parameters; for those, use a [port](/rfx/model/ports/). This page is how to
declare each source, what its amplitude means, and which time signal drives
it.

## How to use it

### Point source

`sim.add_source(position, component, waveform=..., amplitude_kind=...)` drives
`"ex"`, `"ey"` or `"ez"` at the cell nearest `position` (metres). The source is
soft: it adds to the field there and leaves the field free to pass through.

`amplitude_kind` says what the waveform's amplitude is:

| `amplitude_kind` | The amplitude is | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| `"current"` | a current moment I·l in A·m, the strength of a short current element | you want the radiated field to stay the same when you change the mesh |
| `"field"` | an increment of E added at the cell every time step | you need a raw field kick, or the solver path takes only this (ADI) |

Leaving `amplitude_kind` out means `"current"` and gives one
`DeprecationWarning` per simulation. Pass it explicitly.

The difference shows when the mesh changes. The same source and probe on three
cell sizes:

```python
import numpy as np
from rfx import ModulatedGaussian, Simulation


def peak(dx, kind):
    sim = Simulation(freq_max=6e9, domain=(0.06, 0.06, 0.06), dx=dx,
                     boundary="cpml", cpml_layers=8)
    sim.add_source((0.03, 0.03, 0.03), "ez", amplitude_kind=kind,
                   waveform=ModulatedGaussian(f0=3e9, bandwidth=0.5))
    sim.add_probe((0.048, 0.03, 0.03), "ez")
    n = int(round(400 * 2e-3 / dx))          # the same simulation time
    result = sim.run(n_steps=n, skip_preflight=True)
    return float(np.max(np.abs(np.asarray(result.time_series))))


for kind in ("current", "field"):
    print(kind, " ".join(f"{peak(dx, kind):.4g}" for dx in (2e-3, 1.5e-3, 1e-3)))
```

```text
current 1.055e+05 1.043e+05 1.035e+05
field 0.00196 0.00109 0.0004805
```

With `"current"`, the peak field at the probe 18 mm away moves by 1.1 % from
2 mm to 1.5 mm cells and by 0.8 % from 1.5 mm to 1 mm: it converges, as a
physical source should. With `"field"`, adding a fixed E increment to one cell
every step is a current moment proportional to the cell volume over the time
step, so it scales as dx². The peaks follow that: ratios of 0.556 and 0.441
against 0.5625 and 0.444 predicted. The result then depends on the cell size
you happened to choose.

### Polarized source

`sim.add_polarized_source(position, polarization=..., waveform=...,
amplitude_kind=...)` drives Ex and Ey at one point with fixed weights; a single
component (`"ez"`) is the same as `add_source` with that component.
`polarization` is a component (`"ez"`), a name (`"slant45"`, `"rhcp"`,
`"lhcp"`, `"circular"`), or a Jones vector `(Ex, Ey)`, normalized for you. A
complex weight sets the phase of Ey relative to Ex. Circular polarization is
not checked by any test; treat it as unchecked.

### Plane wave

`sim.add_tfsf_source(...)` launches a plane wave along x with the
total-field/scattered-field method: inside a box the field is incident plus
scattered, outside it only the scattered field remains, so what you measure
outside is the structure's response alone.

| Argument | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `direction` | `"+x"` | `"+x"` or `"-x"` |
| `polarization` | `"ez"` | `"ez"` or `"ey"`, the E component of the incident wave; `"s"` or `"p"` for an oblique closed box |
| `angle_deg` | 0 | angle from the x axis, below 90°, for the plane wave without a closed box |
| `theta_deg`, `phi_deg` | 0, 0 | the direction of an oblique wave in a closed box (`closed_box=True`), about x; rfx takes the nearest lattice direction within `direction_tol_deg` (0.1°), and `sim.tfsf_incidence()` reports the direction it realized |
| `method` | `"bloch"` | for oblique incidence: `"bloch"` makes the side axis periodic with the matching phase; `"methodB"` absorbs on it |
| `closed_box` | `False` | `True`: a finite box with six faces around the structure, for a single scatterer; `False`: two planes across the domain |
| `f0`, `bandwidth`, `amplitude` | `freq_max/2`, 0.5, 1 | the pulse |
| `waveform` | `"differentiated_gaussian"` at normal incidence, in a closed box and with `method="methodB"`; `"modulated_gaussian"` at an angle with `method="bloch"` | also `"continuous_wave"` (normal incidence) or a `CustomWaveform` (normal incidence, 3-D). An explicit `"differentiated_gaussian"` at an angle with `method="bloch"` is refused: that method carries one transverse wavenumber, which only a pulse around `f0` matches |
| `margin` | 3 | cells between the injection planes and the absorber |

The injection planes must lie in vacuum. With `closed_box=False` the planes
span the domain, and the faces across the beam are solved as periodic, so the
structure is repeated along them; a structure that is not uniform across them
is refused unless you declare those faces periodic. See
[Boundaries](/rfx/model/boundaries/#rules-that-ports-and-plane-waves-put-on-the-boundary)
for the rule. For a single scatterer use `closed_box=True`.

### Waveforms

| Waveform | What it is | Parameters (defaults) |
|---|---|---|
| `GaussianPulse` | derivative of a Gaussian, no carrier, corrected so that it starts at exactly zero and its time integral is zero | `f0`, `bandwidth=0.5`, `amplitude=1.0`, `cutoff=3.0` (at least 2.5) |
| `ModulatedGaussian` | a carrier at `f0` under a Gaussian envelope | `f0`, `bandwidth=0.5`, `amplitude=1.0`, `cutoff=5.0` |
| `CWSource` | a sine at `f0`, switched on over `ramp_steps` cycles of `f0` (not time steps) | `f0`, `amplitude=1.0`, `ramp_steps=50` |
| `CustomWaveform` | your function of time, `func(t)` with t in seconds | `func` |

`bandwidth` is a fraction of `f0`; the pulse peaks `cutoff` envelope widths
after t = 0. A `bandwidth` given in Hz makes a pulse about 10⁻²⁰ s long that
drives nothing, and the run refuses it. `add_source` and `add_port` use
`GaussianPulse(f0=freq_max/2, bandwidth=0.8)` when you give no waveform.

A pulse gives the response over its whole band from one run. A `CWSource` gives
one frequency and never switches off, so the
[settling check](/rfx/evidence/simulation-time/), which needs the source to be
off before the end of the run, cannot judge it.

Because the default pulse has a zero time integral, a soft current source
driven by it leaves no static charge, and so no static field, behind. The correction that makes this so changes the plain
differentiated Gaussian only before its peak, by at most 1.7×10⁻³ of its
amplitude at `cutoff=3`
(`tests/unit/sources/test_source_dc_floor_guards.py`). A `ModulatedGaussian`
has no such correction; its time integral is small but not zero.

## What is checked

When you declare a source: the component, `amplitude_kind`, and for a plane
wave every argument in the table above against its allowed values and the
faces across the beam. Before the first step, `run()` and `forward()` refuse,
on either mesh and without depending on preflight, a dielectric or a design
region on or next to a plane wave's injection planes, and a soft source on an
edge the grid realizes as metal. Every excited drive's pulse, sampled at the
time step, must exceed 10⁻⁶ of its amplitude somewhere. The solver path then
checks that it implements the source (below).

## What is refused, and the way through

| Input | Solver path | What happens | Instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| `amplitude_kind="current"` | ADI | refused | pass `amplitude_kind="field"` |
| a plane wave together with a lumped port | all | refused | drive the port alone, or use a probe for the response |
| a plane wave with `boundary="upml"`, or in a closed (all-metal) domain | all | refused | `boundary="cpml"` |
| `waveform="continuous_wave"` at an angle | all | refused | a pulse waveform |
| an explicit `waveform="differentiated_gaussian"` at an angle with `method="bloch"`, in Python or in a saved interop document | all | refused | `"modulated_gaussian"` or no `waveform`; for one scatterer in an open domain, `method="methodB"` takes the differentiated Gaussian |
| a `CustomWaveform` plane wave at an angle or in 2-D | all | refused | normal incidence in 3-D |
| `closed_box=True` with `angle_deg`, in 2-D, with a face that is not CPML, on cells that are not cubic, or in a run with no fixed step count | uniform mesh | refused | `theta_deg`/`phi_deg` for the angle, CPML on all six faces, `dx=dy=dz`, `n_steps` |
| `method="methodB"` at normal incidence, with `"ey"`, along `"-x"`, or with a waveform other than the two Gaussians | all | refused | `method="bloch"` |
| a structure that is not uniform across the faces across the beam (`closed_box=False`) | uniform and graded mesh | refused | declare those faces periodic for an array; `closed_box=True` on a uniform mesh for one object |
| a dielectric or a design region on or next to an injection plane | uniform and graded mesh, `run()` and `forward()` | refused before the first step | move the structure clear of the planes |
| a plane wave at an angle, with `closed_box=True`, or with a `CustomWaveform` | graded mesh | refused | use a uniform mesh |
| a plane wave | graded mesh on several devices | refused | run on one device |
| a plane wave | uniform mesh on several devices | runs on one device, with a warning; `Result.diagnostics` records it | — |
| a soft source on an edge the grid realizes as PEC (inside a PEC volume, on a sheet or wire), or tangential in a declared PEC wall face | all | refused | move the source off the metal and at least one cell inside the domain, or use a port |
| a drive whose pulse samples to zero at every time step (usually `bandwidth` given in Hz) | all, and `preflight()` | refused | give `bandwidth` as a fraction of `f0` |
| `GaussianPulse(cutoff=...)` below 2.5 | when the pulse is created | refused | keep the default `cutoff=3` |

[Refused inputs](/rfx/check/refusals/) explains how refusals read; the
[support page](/rfx/support/) lists them per solver path.

## Accuracy

- A plane wave at 30° and at 45° leaks less than 1 % of its energy outside
  the total-field region, and less than 0.1 % at normal incidence, in vacuum on
  a 1 mm mesh at 5 GHz (`tests/unit/sources/test_tfsf_oblique.py`).
- Oblique plane-wave reflection from an εr = 4 slab agrees with the Fresnel
  coefficient within 10 % at 30° and within 8 % at 45°, on a 2 mm mesh at 5 GHz
  (`tests/oracle/test_oblique_fresnel_magnitude.py`).
- A closed-box plane wave in vacuum reproduces the incident pulse inside the
  box, and outside it the field stays below 9 float32 rounding steps of the
  pulse peak (`tests/unit/sources/test_tfsf_closed_box.py`).

### Fixed — a series R+C, R+L or R+L+C lumped element realized about 215 ohm less resistance than declared, and below that it drove the run to NaN (#1163)

- `add_lumped_rlc(..., topology="series")` with two or more of R, L, C took
  its current from the edge field before the element's own current had acted
  on it and subtracted that current afterwards. That removes the edge's own
  impedance d/(D0·A) — 215 ohm in a vacuum cubic cell, at any cell size — from
  R. On a line whose load reflection is a closed form, a series 300 ohm + 1 pF
  read 84.7 ohm + 1/(jωC); 300 ohm + 1 nH read 75 ohm and 0.76 nH. At R below
  about 215 ohm the element was a negative resistance, and a series element
  off a port cell grew to non-finite fields without a warning.
- The element current and the edge field are now solved together in one
  implicit, trapezoidal step. The current enters the field through the
  edge's own E-update coefficient (the four-cell edge average of #1213 plus
  lumped stamps), not the single cell's permittivity. With C → ∞ the step is
  the folded resistor's update. On the same line, series R+C (1 pF, 0.3 pF),
  R+L (1 nH) and R+L+C read their closed-form impedance within ±5 % on every
  one of 91 bins from 1 to 10 GHz. In the 5-cell box of
  `scripts/diagnostics/lumped_rlc_adjacent_to_port_nan.py`, every
  R from 0.1 to 300 ohm with C, L or L+C, at 0.5, 1 and 2 mm cells, decays.
- Results that change: every model with a series multi-component
  `add_lumped_rlc` element. Parallel elements and single-component elements
  (folded into σ / ε) are bit-identical, and so are the lumped/wire battery's
  30 solves.
- `rfx.lumped.update_rlc_element` takes the edge field at the start of the
  step as a fourth argument (`e_prev`). A series element raises without it.

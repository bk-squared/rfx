### Fixed — on a graded mesh, a port's drive sees `eps_override` / `sigma_override`, so a board declared by override is driven like the same board declared as materials, and a permittivity gradient taken through the override is the board's gradient (#1267)

- A wire, single-cell lumped or microstrip port on the graded-mesh lane pushes
  its current into its edges through each edge's update coefficient
  `Cb = dt/(eps + sigma*dt/2)`. The field update read the overridden
  permittivity; the drive was built from the permittivity as drawn, so a port
  whose edges sat in overridden material was driven `Cb_drawn/Cb_override`
  times too hard. A 50 ohm wire port under a layered substrate (eps_r
  3.38/3.38/10.2) supplied by override read S11 0.027 away from the same board
  declared as materials at 8 GHz, and +0.065 dB at 6 GHz on a passive board.
  The two declarations now give identical results.
- `Simulation.forward(eps_override=...)` on the graded lane: a derivative
  through the override carried the derivative of that ratio. On a
  one-material eps_r 3.38 slab under a 50 ohm wire port, raising eps_r by
  e^0.1 through the override scaled every field by 1.036 more than building
  the board with it; the derivative of a probe's ln|E|^2 at 9 GHz with respect
  to ln eps_r read 1.521 against 0.834 from finite differences of built boards
  (the excess, 0.687, is `2*eps/(eps + sigma_port*dt/2)`), and d|S11|^2 with
  respect to one layer's ln eps_r was 1.0 % off. Both now agree with the
  finite differences to 3e-4. The drive is traced when the override is, as on
  the uniform lane.
- Unchanged: every run without an override (byte-identical), the uniform
  lane, waveguide ports, and the microstrip launch fixture's substrate eps_r
  (the static mode shape), which is still read from the drawn materials when
  the port does not state it.

### Fixed — on a graded mesh, a lumped, wire or MSL port now terminates a model that contains a Debye or Lorentz material; before, every such port was an open circuit (#1257, #1283)

- A Debye or Lorentz material anywhere in a graded-mesh model puts the whole
  grid on the dispersive E update, which reads only the coefficients built by
  `init_debye` / `init_lorentz`. The graded lane built them before it stamped
  the port loads, so no port load reached the update. In a 16 mm PEC cavity
  with a 2 mm Debye cube in a corner, the late-time probe amplitude of a 50 ohm
  lumped port was 0.572 of a 5000 ohm port's (the ratio of their two drive
  coefficients), against 0.0074 without the cube. It is now 0.0074. A 2 mm wire
  port went from 0.255 to 0.0064 and an MSL port from 0.789 to 0.167. The
  coefficients are now built after the last port stamp, as on the uniform lane.
- Changes: S-parameters and fields of graded-mesh runs that have both a
  dispersive material and a lumped, wire or MSL port. Runs with no port, no
  dispersive material, or on the uniform lane are unchanged, and so are lumped
  RLC elements, whose fold already came first.

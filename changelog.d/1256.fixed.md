### Fixed — on a graded mesh, a port's drive no longer ignores the port's own 50 ohm load, so the absolute field per unit drive no longer changes with the time step (#1256)

- A wire, single-cell lumped or microstrip port on the graded-mesh lane drives a
  current into the same edges that carry its termination conductance. The drive
  was built from a copy of the materials taken before that conductance was
  stamped, so the edge received `1 + sigma_port*dt/(2*eps)` times the declared
  current: 2.91 for a 50 ohm port across a 1.5 mm, eps_r 3.38 substrate on a
  0.5 mm mesh at the Courant step. A step 0.772 times shorter scaled every
  field, probe and port voltage/current by 0.850. The drive now carries the
  port's own conductance, as on the uniform lane.
- The same copy also missed a lumped R or C (`add_lumped_rlc`) placed across a
  driven edge. With 0.3 pF across the middle edge of a three-cell 50 ohm wire
  port, the graded lane read |S11| = +0.143 dB at 2 GHz (a passive board
  reflecting more than it receives) against −0.005 dB on the uniform lane; the
  two lanes now agree to 1e-6.
- Changes: absolute fields, currents, near-field and far-field levels per unit
  drive on the graded lane. S-parameters are unchanged when all of a port's
  cells share one factor (4 GHz S11 on the board above: 1e-4 between the two
  steps, before and after), and move by up to |ΔS11| 0.025 when they do not:
  on a layered substrate under a wire port (eps_r 3.38/3.38/10.2) the graded
  lane was 0.025 (0.19 dB, 0.9° at 8 GHz) off the uniform lane and now agrees
  to 2e-6; with graded cells under the wire (0.3/0.5/0.7 mm) S11 moves by up
  to 0.0035; an MSL thru moves by at most 1.2e-5.

### Fixed — a conductor that reaches an absorbing boundary now continues through the absorber, as dielectrics already did (#801)

- rfx adds the absorber cells outside the declared domain and used to continue
  only dielectrics into them. A grounded board that ran to the boundary
  therefore became an ungrounded slab inside the absorber, and a lossless patch
  on it GAINED energy with six absorber layers or fewer. With the ground
  continued the same five rigs ring down to −43.0 … −45.6 dB (150 periods,
  float32); before this change three of them sat at 0 dB and grew.
- What continues: a conducting Box, sheet or thin conductor, and a cylinder
  along its own axis, when it is drawn to the declared domain face or fills
  the last cell layer before the absorber. A domain length that is not a whole
  number of cells no longer leaves a bare cell between such a conductor and
  the absorber.
- What does not: the two conductors a port bridges never enter an absorber
  together. On a face both reach, the one with the smaller cross-section (an
  MSL strip, the trace under a wire or coaxial-probe port) keeps its declared
  end and the other (the ground) continues; a PEC boundary face counts as the
  larger partner. Lines therefore stay ended by their ports, and the ground
  under a probe-fed patch, whose patch reaches no face, continues everywhere.
  Spheres, cross-axis cylinders, meshes and wires that reach an absorbing face
  are not continued and preflight says so. With a traced mesh axis nothing is
  continued and one warning says so.
- Preflight and the fidelity report still compare the DRAWN size with the
  solved size inside the declared domain; the continued part is named on its
  own line.

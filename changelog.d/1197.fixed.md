### Fixed — the occupancy-to-tensor builder of the Kottke PEC lane put the conductor one cell past the occupancy; a binary PEC slab now leaves a cavity's TM110 where it is (#1197)

- `kottke_inv_eps_from_occupancy` (the `RFX_PEC_OCC_KOTTKE=1` lane behind
  `pec_occupancy_override`) built a cell-centred fill with a `grad(occ)`
  normal, then dilated it by one cell in all six directions with a
  Heaviside at 0.5. The dilation wrote `inv = 0` into the first air cell
  beyond a conductor, including the NORMAL component there — where a cavity
  mode's normal E is largest. A 24 mm PEC cube with a binary PEC slab read
  TM110 at 8.5266 GHz (−3.5 %; analytic 8.833, `apply_pec_occupancy` 8.8305);
  with the slab edge at a cell centre 8.3228 GHz (−5.8 %).
- The tensor is now the E-update form of the lattice ownership contract:
  each E edge is conductor to the degree the four cells sharing it are
  occupied (`_volume_occupancy_masks`, §1.2/§1.6), `inv = (1 − M) × baseline`.
  At binary occupancy the zeros are `realized_pec_edge_masks` bit for bit
  (pinned on a 12-edge box); the binary slab reads 8.8305 GHz
  (`tests/oracle/test_kottke_occupancy_cavity.py`). Fractional occupancy is a
  lossless interpolation (a static high-permittivity cell, Q 1e7), not the
  per-step decay of the scaling lane; it still shifts a mode (−3.6 % with the
  edge at a cell centre) and is a continuation, exact at 0 and 1.
- The dilation had been kept on an |S21| witness of one notch run (`60939e0`,
  2026-05-10); that board's stub sat a cell off the line (#1182), so the
  witness was not measuring the tensor.

### Changed — `run(ringdown=...)`: the completed S-matrix is checked against a completion from a window that starts twice as early, so some short records that read ok before now read not ok (#1254)

- The report's error witness is now `WE`: the completion from the main
  window [T/2, T] against the completion from [T/4, T], a window that starts
  twice as early and contains the main one; the largest |S| difference over
  every bin and entry, bar `witness_tol` (1e-3). The previous witness `W2`
  ([T/2, T] against [T/2, 0.9 T]) is still computed and reported, but no
  longer decides `report.ok` (`RingdownWitness.judged` is False for it;
  `report.ok` reads the judged witnesses only). The two windows of `W2`
  share their start and most of their samples, so a pole pair both of them
  fail to separate leaves them in agreement while the completion is wrong:
  on a 3.64 ns record of two Q 400 modes 0.1 % apart
  (`tests/unit/sparams/test_ringdown_oracles.py`), with the main window
  starting at 0.6 T the completion is off by 1.17 of the spectrum's peak,
  `W2` reads 0.0024 of that error and `WE` 1.01 of it. `WE` is not a bound:
  it can read somewhat below the error, so a record slightly over the bar
  can still read ok.
- When a source is still on in [T/4, T], WE is not formed: the report
  lists it as not formed and judges W2 in its place, with a note saying so.
- The completed S itself (`Result.ringdown.s_params`) is unchanged, bit for
  bit, and so is every other output.
- Some records that read ok before now read not ok. On the 12 × 11 × 5 mm
  metal box graded 0.6-1.4 mm on all axes (TM110 at 12.40 GHz), a 1.72 ns
  record (1500 steps) completes S11 to 1.65e-3 of the completed 30,000-step
  answer: WE reads 1.6e-3 and fails it; W2 read 3.0e-4 and passed it under
  JAX 0.10.2 (1.7e-3 under JAX 0.4.33). At 2.86 ns (2500 steps) the error is
  8.9e-5 and WE reads 9.8e-5.
- The added identification on the longer window costs time that grows with
  the record: seconds on records of a few thousand steps, minutes on a
  40,000-step record.
